2. Abstract: Radiation source identification (SEI) is a technology based on the inherent characteristics of individual radiation sources reflected in radio frequency signals for recognition. Deep learning (DL) methods have become the main approach for SEI, widely applied in wireless device recognition and authentication. However, the differences in electromagnetic signals under different environments lead to significant performance degradation in unseen domains, making traditional deep learning methods difficult to address this challenge. To alleviate the issue of limited performance in SEI under different scenarios, this paper proposes a hybrid enhancement method TFMix, which integrates time-domain and frequency-domain data enhancement techniques. Specifically, TFMix first generates enhanced samples through time-domain interpolation (TDI), followed by Fourier transformation; in the frequency domain, the method performs amplitude interpolation mixing with controllable phase perturbations, and finally reconstructs more robust signal samples through inverse Fourier transformation. This hybrid strategy generates diverse and richly informative training samples, effectively enhancing the model's generalization capability. To verify the effectiveness of TFMix, evaluations were conducted on datasets from various scenarios, with experimental results showing that TFMix significantly outperforms existing DG methods in cross-scene generalization performance, demonstrating outstanding robustness and generalization capabilities in SEI tasks.

Introduction: In recent years, the rapid development of information technology has led to an explosion in the variety and quantity of wireless devices, deeply integrating wireless networks into multiple fields such as mobile communication and Internet of Things (IoT). However, the openness and complexity of wireless channels pose serious security risks such as signal hijacking, interference, and deception attacks, making it increasingly important to ensure the security and reliability of wireless communications. This also raises higher requirements for effective identification and authentication of wireless devices.
The Specific Emission Source Identification (SEI) technology, through the analysis of distinctively weak signals characteristically difficult to replicate, distinguishes different emission sources in both civilian and military applications. Deep learning-based SEI techniques can automatically extract features from signals, significantly enhancing recognition accuracy. However, these methods typically assume that the data is independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.), a condition often not met in real-world scenarios, where deviations in data distribution can lead to decreased model performance.
In recent years, transfer learning, especially domain adaptation (DA), has made significant progress in SEI. DA techniques learn the differences between domains by transferring knowledge from labeled data in the source domain to unlabeled data in the target domain, improving recognition performance in the target domain. For example, Ye et al. proposed an adversarial DA method based on Wasserstein distance for unsupervised SEI, Shi et al. introduced a cross-domain SEI algorithm with domain-adversarial multi-constraints, and Wan et al. introduced a semi-supervised DA method based on global and local semantic consistency to handle channel changes. However, these DA methods have limitations: most require obtaining target domain data during training, which conflicts with the scenario where it is difficult to collect target domain data in actual SEI applications, and training models for each target domain is costly and time-consuming.
To address this challenge, domain generalization (DG) has emerged. Unlike DA, DG does not require obtaining target domain data during training, aiming to build models that can directly generalize to unseen domains, which is crucial for SEI—collecting labeled data from all potential work environments is neither realistic nor feasible. Existing DG methods mostly focus on signal acquisition and preprocessing, but they face high complexity and large computational resource consumption issues. Additionally, DG research has mostly concentrated on image recognition tasks, with relatively few DG methods specifically tailored for SEI, thus urgently needing the development of DG techniques specifically suitable for SEI.
This paper proposes a novel robust SEI method, TFMix, which innovatively integrates time-domain and frequency-domain enhancement techniques. By mixing and interpolating signals from different source domains to generate new synthetic samples, it enhances the generalization capability of models in SEI tasks. The main contributions of this paper include: 1) introducing the TFMix method that combines time-domain interpolation (TDI) with frequency-domain Fourier transforms, utilizing time-domain operations to capture temporal patterns and frequency-domain techniques to enhance spectral features, thereby improving the diversity and robustness of signal samples; 2) introducing phase perturbations on amplitude interpolation to generate samples with richer feature representations, enhancing the model's adaptability to signal changes; 3) validating the effectiveness of TFMix based on the WiSig dataset across time and receivers, with experimental results showing that this method outperforms other existing methods in terms of DG-SEI performance.
IV. Method Introduction The TFMix method focuses on enhancing the integration of time and frequency domains, encompassing four core components: signal data enhancement, time-domain interpolation (TDI), frequency domain enhancement, and model training.
1  
Overall Framework: A complex value neural network (CVNN) is used as the feature extractor to capture the coupling information of I and Q components in radio frequency signals. The feature extractor consists of nine complex convolutional layers, while the classifier consists of three fully connected layers. During the training process, data augmentation techniques are integrated. During testing, unseen domain datasets are input into the optimized neural network for classification predictions.
2  
Signal Data Augmentation: Drawing on the Neighborhood Risk Minimization (VRM) principle of Mixup, generates virtual training samples through linear interpolation, but adapts to the characteristics of radio frequency signals, avoiding the direct application of traditional image enhancement methods.
3  
Temporal Interpolation (TDI): Select two signal samples from different source domains randomly, perform linear interpolation based on dynamically adjusted interpolation coefficients to generate new samples with richer features. At the same time, mix the labels accordingly, enhancing both sample diversity and providing a richer feature space for deep learning models.
4  
Frequency Domain Enhancement: First, the time-domain signal is converted into a frequency domain representation through Fourier transform, extracting amplitude and phase information. The amplitude is interpolated mixed (based on random sampling coefficients), and random noise perturbations are introduced to the phase. Subsequently, the perturbed phase and interpolated amplitude are used to reconstruct the frequency domain signal, which is then transformed back to the time domain through inverse Fourier transform to obtain the enhanced signal samples after time-frequency domain joint enhancement.
Training Process: The loss function is minimized using the Adam optimizer, based on the mixed label calculation of negative log likelihood loss. The forward propagation generates enhanced samples and predicts class distribution, while the backward propagation updates model parameters to complete the model training.
Conclusion: This paper proposes a robust SEI method based on TFMix, significantly enhancing the generalization performance of SEI in unknown domains. The method generates samples through time-domain interpolation, performs amplitude interpolation mixing and phase perturbation after Fourier transform, and finally generates more robust signal samples through inverse Fourier transform, enhancing the model's generalization capability with diverse training samples.
However, TFMix still has certain limitations: If the dataset contains strong noise, the interpolation process may amplify the noise, reducing the model's generalization performance. Therefore, effective preprocessing and denoising techniques are required; the method relies on the selection of the mixing ratio parameter, and different datasets and tasks require adjustment of parameters, increasing the complexity of parameter tuning.
Despite these challenges, TFMix still demonstrates significant advantages in both generalization and accuracy, making it an effective practical solution for SEI. Future work will focus on optimizing the parameter tuning process of TFMix, developing automated techniques for dynamically selecting the mixing ratio; meanwhile, improving the TFMix algorithm to enhance its adaptability to different signal data types and input formats, further strengthening the robustness and generalization capabilities of the SEI model.
